Data Cleaning and Preparation¶

The outcome of this process was a clean analytical dataset suitable for both exploratory analysis and regression modeling.

Model Performance¶

The negative R² on the test set indicates that the model performed worse than simply predicting the average sales volume for all products. With average sales around 1,824 units, an RMSE of nearly 700 units reflects large errors relative to actual product performance.

Visual inspection of predicted versus actual sales shows that the model consistently regresses toward the mean, failing to differentiate high-performing from low-performing SKUs. This is characteristic of underfitting, confirming that the selected attributes do not sufficiently explain demand variation.

Importantly, the weak predictive performance offers a deeper business insight: the primary drivers of fashion demand—such as trendiness, design features, imagery, fit, and social media influence—are not captured in the dataset and therefore cannot be modeled using these basic catalog features alone.

Despite the low predictive accuracy, the model coefficients reveal meaningful directional insights:

  • Products in certain sections, particularly WOMAN, tend to achieve slightly higher sales on average.
  • Product placement (front-of-store) correlates with higher sales in raw data but has only a modest independent effect after controlling for other variables.
  • Promotions are negatively associated with sales, likely because discounted products were underperformers before markdowns.
  • Price has little explanatory power for demand within Zara’s fast-fashion model.

Business Recommendations:¶

Drawing on both EDA and regression insights, several actionable recommendations emerge for Zara’s merchandising, assortment planning, and forecasting processes.

Strengthen High-Performing Sections¶

Given that section is the strongest predictor of sales differences, Zara can expand SKU depth and marketing focus in categories showing high per-SKU sales (particularly WOMAN). Targeted investment in these areas is likely to yield higher returns.

Redesign Promotion Strategy¶

The negative relationship between promotions and sales suggests inefficiencies in current markdown processes. Zara should adopt early detection of slow-moving products and apply smaller, more targeted discounts rather than relying on heavy markdowns later in the cycle.

Optimize Digital and In-Store Product Placement¶

Although placement shows limited effect in regression, EDA confirms strong visibility-related patterns. Zara can leverage digital algorithms to prioritize high-potential items in prominent display areas, both online and offline.

Improve Seasonal Forecasting¶

Weaker seasonal performance suggests a need for better forecasting techniques. Integrating climate data, historical seasonal trends, and regional demand variation can help Zara refine production and distribution planning.

Expand Future Data Collection¶

The model’s limitations point to missing variables critical for fashion forecasting. Zara can significantly improve predictive accuracy by incorporating additional data:

  • Image-based attributes (color, silhouette, design features)
  • Social media engagement and influencer mentions
  • Real-time browsing data (views, add-to-cart events)
  • Stock availability and replenishment cycles These elements are essential for any future predictive modeling or trend forecasting initiatives.
